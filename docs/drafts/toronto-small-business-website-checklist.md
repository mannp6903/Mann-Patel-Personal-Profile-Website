# DRAFT, not published: Website checklist for Toronto service businesses

**Who this is for:** owners of cleaning, trades, maintenance and other service businesses in
Toronto who want to know whether their website is doing its job, without the jargon.

## 1. Can a stranger tell what you do and where, in five seconds?

- The first screen names the service and the area you actually cover (for example "House cleaning
  in Scarborough and North York"), not a slogan.
- One page per service, each answering what it includes.
- **Example needed:** the first screen of a real project, with its date.

## 2. Does it work on a phone?

- No sideways scrolling at common phone widths (360 and 390 pixels).
- Text is readable without zooming; buttons are big enough for a thumb.
- The phone number is a tap-to-call link.
- **Example needed:** the phone-width check recorded on a case study.

## 3. Is the price, or how quotes work, easy to find?

- Show a price, a starting price, or the steps to a quote. Silence makes people leave.
- Say what changes the price.
- **Example needed:** how a real project shows its price or quote steps.

## 4. Is there one clear way to enquire, and has anyone tested it?

- One main path: a short form, a call or a booking link, on every page.
- Send a test enquiry yourself. Did you get it? Did the customer get a confirmation?
- If the form fails, does it say so and keep what was typed?
- A booking **request** is not a booking: say which one your page offers.

## 5. Can Google read it?

- Each page has its own title and a one-sentence description.
- A sitemap exists and Search Console is verified in your name.
- Your Google Business Profile shows the same name, phone number and hours as your website.

## 6. Do you own it?

- The domain, hosting and every account are in your name.
- You have a login list and a short "how to edit" note.
- You know what you pay each provider, and when.

## 7. The seven checks I run before any site goes live

1. The enquiry form, end to end
2. Tap-to-call on a phone
3. Layout at 360, 390, 768 and 1440 pixels
4. An automated accessibility scan (axe, WCAG 2.2 A and AA rules), with anything found listed
5. Titles, descriptions and a sitemap
6. Search Console verified in the owner's name
7. A written handover
