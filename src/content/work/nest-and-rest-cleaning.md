## The situation

A new cleaning business starts with no reviews, no name recognition and a crowded local market. People choosing a cleaner on their phone want three answers quickly: **what will it cost, do you come to my area, and can I trust the person coming into my home?** Nest & Rest had to answer all three before anyone had heard of it, and it had to do that honestly while the business is still getting ready.

## Who it's for

Busy households in Scarborough and North York, in condos, townhouses and houses, plus Airbnb hosts who need same-day turnovers. Most arrive on a phone.

## Scope

- Six home services with prices shown up front, plus Airbnb turnovers, residence care, small offices and a page for realtors
- The website: home page, a page per service, a pricing page with a room-by-room comparison, Scarborough and North York pages with 12 neighbourhood pages, a three-step booking request, gift cards, five guides, a vision page with always/never promises, service policies and a privacy policy
- Ways to get in touch: email now; the booking request, quote forms and “text me this price” are built and switch on at launch
- Search and AI visibility: every page answers its question first, and the business, its prices and areas are described in the page data for Google and AI assistants

## Decisions that shaped it

1. **Price first.** The first screen shows a real price for your service, home type, bedrooms and bathrooms, without asking for a name, email or phone. Some local competitors ask for contact details before they show a price.
2. **Show exactly what's included.** One room-by-room checklist decides what each service includes, what is a paid extra and what isn't offered, so the price box, the service pages and the booking form can never disagree.
3. **The owner is the brand.** "Hi, I'm Mann. I clean every home myself." With no reviews yet, the proof is the person at the door, with his photo and name.
4. **Believable areas.** Two districts, with neighbourhood pages and a postal-code check, instead of claiming the whole GTA.
5. **Honest about timing.** Until bookings open, the site says "Opening soon" on every page and takes no requests. One setting pauses every form; prices, services and guides stay up so people can see what to expect. When the business is ready, the same setting brings everything back at once.

## How it evolved

The archived 19 October version had the estimator, services and a visit standard. By 31 October the site had added Airbnb turnovers, gift cards, the room-by-room checklist, a pricing page, guides and neighbourhood pages. In the first week of November it was redesigned in four steps: answer-first pages, new type and a colour for each service, the signature moments, and local neighbourhood pages. Then came a photo hero of Toronto's skyline and the CN Tower at dawn, and "Opening soon" mode. An AI chat assistant is built and stays off until it is deployed.

## Results

None are claimed here. The website went live in October 2026, and the business starts taking clients in summer 2027.
