## The brief (fictional)

Northline Plumbing is an imagined two-van plumbing business in the GTA. The owner is under a sink most of the day. Enquiries arrive by phone, Instagram and an old contact form that emails an address nobody checks. Some customers wait a day for a reply, and some never get one.

## The approach

1. **A form that asks what the plumber needs:** the problem, the postal code, how urgent it is, and the best way to reply. Photos are optional. The form doesn't ask for anything that isn't used.
2. **An honest acknowledgement:** "Got it — this is a request, not a booked visit. I reply to every request by 8 a.m. the next working day, sooner for urgent leaks." The promised time is the owner's real routine, agreed before it's written.
3. **One place to look:** every request becomes a row in a lead list (a shared spreadsheet is enough to start) with a status: *New → Replied → Booked → Done*, or *Not a fit*.
4. **A safety net:** if a request is still *New* the next morning, the owner gets a reminder.

## Business Profile

A plumber who travels to customers is a service-area business. The profile should hide the home address, list the real service areas, and use the real business name, with no keywords stuffed in. The sample includes that checklist.

## What a real project would add

Testing with the owner's real phone and inbox, the real reply times, connection to the calendar they already use, and a review of the old form so nothing is lost during the switch.
