A website redesign of up to five pages costs $1,950 CAD with me, before tax: it's the same Website Launch package as a brand-new site, with a fixed scope written down before any work starts. If only one thing on your current site is broken, a Website Fix costs $450–$850, depending on the size of the fix, and leaves the rest of the site alone. I publish my prices, so this guide uses them as a worked example of what a redesign covers and what changes the price.

## Redesign or repair?

The cheapest redesign is the one you don't need. Start by asking what is actually costing you customers.

- **A repair is enough** when one thing fails: a contact form that doesn't reach you, a booking button customers can't find on a phone, an outdated menu or price list, or one slow page. That's a [Website Fix](/pricing/#pkg-website-fix), $450–$850.
- **A redesign makes sense** when several pages are hard to use on a phone, the site doesn't say what you do, where you work and how to book, or no one can edit it because the logins are lost or not in your name. That's [Website Launch](/pricing/#pkg-website-launch), $1,950.

The [free website and Google check](/contact/?package=check-up#check) answers this for your site: I look at it the way a customer would, on a phone and a laptop, and send a short written list of what fails, most important first.

## What $1,950 covers in a redesign

- up to five pages, rebuilt for phones first: usually Home, Services, About, Contact and one service or area page
- one enquiry path, such as a quote or contact form, that confirms to the customer and reaches your inbox, tested end to end
- click-to-call and one clear next step on every page
- search basics: page titles and descriptions, a sitemap, and Search Console and Analytics in your name
- two rounds of revisions
- a handover with every login in your name, a short editing guide and the test results

It's paid in two halves: half at kickoff, and half once the agreed launch checks pass. All prices are in Canadian dollars, plus applicable tax.

## What a redesign keeps

- **Your domain.** The new site goes live on the address you already own, and the domain stays in your name with your registrar.
- **The words and photos that still work.** I edit the text you already have for clarity, rather than start from nothing.
- **Your Google Business Profile.** A redesign doesn't touch it, though it's a good time to check that its website link points to the right page.

## What changes the price

- **More than five pages.** Extra pages are quoted in writing on top.
- **Writing from scratch.** I edit what you supply. Writing all of your copy from nothing is quoted separately.
- **Online booking and follow-up.** Connecting a booking tool, or adding a workflow such as missed-call text-back, makes it the [Website + Enquiry System](/pricing/#pkg-website-enquiry) package, $3,350, instead.
- **The state of the old site.** Broken forms or a lost login can need sorting out before the new work starts.
- **Things no package includes:** online stores, photography and logo design.

## What you pay other providers

Some costs never go through me. Each is billed to you directly, at the provider's own rates, and named in your written scope before you decide:

- your domain name, every year, from your registrar
- hosting, if your chosen host charges for it
- email at your domain, if you want it
- your booking tool's subscription, if you add one

## Questions to ask before any redesign

Whoever you hire, ask these before you sign:

1. Exactly what's included: how many pages, how many rounds of revisions, which forms? Is it written down?
2. Who will own the domain, the hosting and every login afterwards? They should all be in your name.
3. What happens to the old site's page addresses? Will links and bookmarks to them still lead somewhere useful?
4. Is the enquiry form tested end to end before launch, and do you get the results?
5. What isn't included, and what will other providers bill you each year?
6. Does anyone promise you a position on Google? Google decides rankings, so no one can promise one.
