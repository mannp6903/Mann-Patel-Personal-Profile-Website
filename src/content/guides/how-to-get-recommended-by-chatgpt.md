People now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot for "a house cleaner near me" or "a good salon in Scarborough". No one can make an AI assistant recommend your business, and anyone who promises that is guessing. What you can do is make accurate facts about your business easy to find, read and quote. This guide covers where those facts come from and what to fix first.

## Where AI answers get facts about local businesses

AI assistants that search the web answer from what they can find and read. For a local business, that is mostly:

- your Google Business Profile, Apple Maps and Bing listings
- your own website
- directories and review sites, and what customers write in reviews

Each tool gets there differently:

- **ChatGPT search** finds websites with OpenAI's crawler, OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI says sites that block OAI-SearchBot won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers.
- **Microsoft Copilot** grounds its answers in Bing search results, so a business Bing doesn't know about is hard for Copilot to mention.
- **Google AI Overviews** are part of Google Search. Google says there are no extra requirements to appear in them: the same things that help a page in Search apply.

The common thread: if search engines can't reach your website, or your listings disagree with each other, AI answers have little to go on, or repeat the mistakes.

## The checklist

1. **Let the crawlers in.** Make sure your website doesn't block OAI-SearchBot, Bingbot or Googlebot in its robots.txt file. OpenAI notes that a firewall or bot-protection setting can block its crawler even when robots.txt allows it, so check those too if you use them.
2. **Verify your website in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools,** and submit your sitemap to both. Bing Webmaster Tools can import your site from Search Console.
3. **Use the same facts everywhere.** Your business name, phone number, hours, website and service area should match on your Google Business Profile, Apple Maps (through Apple Business Connect), Bing Places, Facebook and any directory. Wrong hours in one listing are exactly the kind of fact an assistant repeats.
4. **Answer real questions on your website, in plain words.** What you do, where, what it costs or how quotes work, and how to book. One page per service. An assistant can only quote what is written down.
5. **Describe your business details and questions with structured data** (schema.org). Google says it isn't required for its AI features, but it states your name, phone, area and FAQs in a form machines read without guessing. Keep it matching what the page shows.
6. **Collect reviews that describe the work,** and reply to them. Ask every customer the same way after each job. Never buy, swap or filter reviews: Google forbids incentives, and Canada's Competition Bureau treats fake reviews as misleading advertising.

## How to check what AI assistants say about you

- Write down three questions a customer would really ask, for example "Who cleans condos in North York and shows prices?"
- Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity each question.
- Log the date, the tool, the question, whether your business appeared, whether it linked your website, and which facts it used.
- Repeat the same questions every month or two. Answers change between tools and from day to day, so compare the log over months, not one answer.

If an assistant gets a fact wrong, find where that fact is published and fix it there: usually a listing, an old directory entry or your own website.

## What not to do

- Don't pay anyone who promises a place in AI answers. No one controls what an assistant says.
- Don't publish invented reviews, awards or claims. Assistants repeat what they find, including what isn't true.
- Don't put key facts only in images. A price list in a photo is harder for search engines to read than the same prices written on the page.
