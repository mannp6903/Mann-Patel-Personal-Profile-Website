To optimize your Google Business Profile, get every detail exactly right and keep the profile active: your real business name, the most specific category, accurate hours and service area, your services with prices, real photos, a post every week or two, and genuine reviews that you reply to. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence ([Google: how local results are ranked](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). You can't move closer to every customer, but you can make your profile more relevant and more prominent. Here is the checklist I use, in order.

## 1. Check you're eligible, then verify

A Business Profile is for a business that meets its customers in person, at its own address or at theirs ([Google: guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)). Online-only businesses don't qualify. If you work from home or travel to customers, you can hide your home address: the guide to a Google Business Profile without a storefront, under "Read next", explains how.

Then verify the profile. Many features, such as replying to reviews, only work once it's verified ([Google: manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)).

## 2. Name: your real one, nothing added

Use the name customers see on your sign, website and invoices. Google's guidelines say the name should reflect your real-world business name, so a business called "Example Cleaning" can't become "Example Cleaning Toronto Best House Cleaners" ([Google: guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)). Extra words break the guidelines, and customers can tell.

## 3. The most specific category

Your primary category tells Google what kind of business you are. Google's advice is to choose the most specific category that fits, for example "Nail salon" rather than "Salon" ([Google: choose a business category](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en)). Add secondary categories only for services you really offer. Changing a category can ask you to verify again, so choose carefully.

## 4. Service area and hours

- **Service-area businesses** (cleaners, trades, mobile services) list the areas they serve and hide their address. Google allows up to 20 service areas, and asks that the whole area stays within about two hours' drive of your base ([Google: manage your service area](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en)). List the parts of Toronto you really serve, such as Scarborough, North York or East York, not every city in Ontario.
- **Hours:** set your regular hours, and add holiday hours before long weekends like Victoria Day and Thanksgiving, so no one turns up to a closed door.
- **No virtual offices or mailboxes.** Google's guidelines don't accept P.O. boxes or mailboxes at a remote location as a business address ([Google: guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)).

## 5. Services, with prices if you publish them

Add each service you sell, in the words customers use ("deep cleaning", "balayage"), with a one-line description. If your website shows prices, use the same ones here, so a customer sees one number everywhere.

## 6. A description in plain words

Say what you do, who for and where, in two or three sentences. Write it for a customer, not for a search engine: a list of keywords reads badly and helps no one.

## 7. Real photos

Add photos of your real work, your team and, if customers visit, your premises. Google asks for photos that are in focus and well lit, without heavy filters or alterations ([Google: manage your Business Profile photos](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en)). Never use stock photos passed off as your own work. Aim for ten or more to start, then add new ones as you finish jobs.

## 8. Posts, every week or two

Posts can share updates, offers and events on Search and Maps ([Google: create posts on your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en)). Keep it simple: a finished job with a photo, a seasonal reminder, a short answer to a question customers ask. A profile with recent posts shows customers you're open and active.

## 9. Reviews, and your replies

Google says review count and review score factor into local ranking ([Google: how local results are ranked](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). Ask every customer with your own review link, never offer anything in return, and reply to every review. The full routine is in the guide to getting more Google reviews, under "Read next".

## 10. The same details everywhere

Your name, phone number and website should match, character for character, on your profile, your website, Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp and Facebook. Link the profile to the most useful page on your website, such as your booking or quote page, not only the home page.

## A 30-minute check, every month

- Hours, phone number and website link still right?
- Any new reviews without a reply?
- A post in the last two weeks?
- New photos from recent jobs?
- Any change to your details that you didn't make? Check it and correct it.
