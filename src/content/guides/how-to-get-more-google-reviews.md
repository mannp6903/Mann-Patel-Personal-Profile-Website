To get more Google reviews, ask every customer, every time, with your own review link, soon after the job is done, and reply to the reviews you get. Google allows all of that. What it doesn't allow is paying or rewarding people for reviews, asking only the customers you expect to be happy, or reviews of your own business by you, your staff or your family. Everything below sticks to Google's own help pages, linked as you go.

## What Google allows

- **Asking customers for a review.** Google's own tips suggest reminding customers that leaving a review is quick and easy, and sending them a link to your profile ([Google: tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7035772?hl=en)).
- **Sharing your review link or QR code.** Your Business Profile gives you a link that opens the review form for your business, and a QR code for printed cards ([Google: give customers a link or QR code to review your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)).
- **Replying to reviews as the owner,** once your business is verified ([Google: manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)).

## What Google doesn't allow

Google's policy for reviews and other contributions lists these among the things you can't do ([Google: prohibited and restricted content](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)):

- **Rewards for reviews.** No discount, free extra or prize draw in return for a review. Google's tips page says it plainly: business owners shouldn't offer incentives to customers to leave reviews.
- **Asking only happy customers.** Selectively asking for positive reviews, or discouraging negative ones, is against the policy. Send the same request to everyone.
- **Reviews with a conflict of interest,** such as reviews of your own business by you, your staff or your family.
- **Paid reviews.** The policy bans reviews paid for directly or in kind.

None of these are worth the risk. A smaller number of real reviews is still yours to keep.

## Where to find your review link

1. Open your Business Profile: search for your business name on Google while signed in to the account that manages it, or open it in Google Maps.
2. Choose **Ask for reviews** (on some screens: **Read reviews**, then **Get more reviews**).
3. Copy the link, or download the QR code. Google says the QR code is made on a computer, not on a phone ([Google: link or QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)).

Use this link, not a general Google Maps link to your business: it opens the review form straight away, which saves your customer two taps.

## When and how to ask

Ask when the work is done and the customer has seen the result: at the door after a cleaning, at the chair after a haircut, or in the email that sends the final invoice. Make it one tap.

Places Google itself suggests: receipts, thank-you emails and the end of a chat with the customer ([Google: link or QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)). Add a small printed card with the QR code if you meet customers in person.

A sample message you can adapt:

> Hi Priya, thanks for choosing us today. If you have a minute, a Google review helps other people nearby find us: [your review link]. Thank you!

Send it to every customer, including the one whose appointment ran late. Ask once, and follow up once at most.

## Reply to every review

- **Good reviews:** thank the person, mention what they mentioned, and keep it short.
- **Bad reviews:** stay calm, stick to the facts, and offer to sort it out by phone or email. Never share a customer's personal details in a reply.
- **A review that breaks Google's policy** (spam, or someone who was never a customer): you can flag it for Google to check ([Google: manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en)). Google decides whether it comes down.

Replies are public, so write them for the next customer reading, not only for the reviewer.

## Do reviews help you show up on Google?

Google says review count and review score factor into local ranking, as part of how prominent your business is ([Google: how local results are ranked](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). Google doesn't publish a target number, and no one can promise a position. Reviews also help after you're found: they're often what someone reads before they call.

## A routine you can keep

- **Every job:** send your review link the same day.
- **Every week:** reply to any new reviews.
- **Every month:** check your profile still has the right hours, services and photos. The guide to optimizing your Google Business Profile, under "Read next", has the full checklist.
