If you clean homes, fix things or run your business from home, you can still have a Google Business Profile and show up on Google Maps. You set it up as a **service-area business**: your home address stays hidden, and Google shows the areas you serve instead.

## Can you have a profile without a storefront?

Yes, if you meet your customers in person, at their home or business, during the hours you post. Google's guidelines are written for businesses that make in-person contact with customers. Among the businesses that aren't eligible:

- online-only businesses, which never meet customers in person
- a business that uses a virtual office, mailbox or other address it doesn't actually work from, just to appear in a neighbourhood
- a business that hasn't opened yet: Google only lets you add a future opening date shortly before you open

The [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) list the rest.

If customers also come to you, for example to a studio at your home, the address can be shown as well as your service area. If you always go to them, keep it hidden.

## Setting it up as a service-area business

1. **Use your real business name**, the same one on your website, invoices and signs. Don't add a neighbourhood or keywords that aren't part of the name.
2. **Choose the primary category** that says what you do, for example "House cleaning service". You can add a few secondary categories for other work you really do.
3. **Say you don't have a location customers visit.** Google may still ask for your address to verify the business, but it isn't shown to the public.
4. **Add your service areas:** the parts of the city you actually serve, for example Scarborough and North York.
5. **Fill in the rest:** your hours, services, phone number, a link to your website's quote or booking page, and photos of your real work.
6. **Verify the business.** Google chooses how, and it can take days or longer. Until it is verified, the profile may not appear.

## Make the rest of the web match

Google, AI assistants and customers all compare what they find. Use the same business name, phone number, hours and website on:

- Apple Maps, through Apple Business Connect
- Bing Places
- your Facebook Page and any directory you're listed in
- your own website, with a page for each service and the areas you serve

## Reviews without a storefront

You don't need a shop to collect reviews. Ask every customer the same way after each job, with your review link or a QR card, and reply to every review, including the critical ones. Never buy, swap or filter reviews, and don't offer discounts for them: Google forbids incentives, and Canada's Competition Bureau treats fake reviews as misleading advertising.
