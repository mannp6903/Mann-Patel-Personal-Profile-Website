You search for your business on Google Maps and it isn't there, or it only appears when you type its exact name. Work through this checklist in order: the most common problems are near the top.

## 1. Search the way a customer does

On a phone, search Google Maps for your business name and your city. Then search for what you do and where, for example "house cleaning North York". Showing up for your name but not for your service is a different problem from not showing up at all: skip to step 5 if that's you.

## 2. Is there a profile, and is it verified?

A new Business Profile has to be verified before it appears. Google chooses how, and it can take days or longer. If a profile for your business already exists and someone else manages it, such as a former employee or an old agency, Google shows that you need to request access from them.

## 3. Is your business eligible?

Google Business Profiles are for businesses that meet customers in person, at the business or at the customer's place. Online-only businesses aren't eligible, and neither is an address you don't actually work from, such as a virtual office or mailbox. Not open yet? Google shows a new profile only in the 90 days before the opening date you set.

## 4. Is the profile suspended, or a duplicate?

- A profile that breaks Google's guidelines can be suspended, and it doesn't show while it is. Google tells the account that manages the profile, and you can appeal.
- If Google treats your profile as a duplicate of another one, it's hidden from Search and Maps. Google's help explains how to resolve duplicates and ownership problems.

## 5. Distance, relevance and prominence

Google says local results depend mainly on three things: relevance (how well a profile matches the search), distance (how far the business is from the person searching) and prominence (how well known it is). Someone searching from across the city will often see businesses closer to them first. Google also says you can't pay for a better local ranking.

## 6. Is your primary category right?

The primary category tells Google what your business is. If it doesn't match what people search for, you're less relevant for that search. Pick the category that describes your main service, then add a few secondary ones for other work you really do.

## 7. Is the profile complete?

Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are easier to match with the right searches. Fill in your hours, services, service area, phone number and website, and add photos of your real work.

## 8. Do your details match everywhere?

Your business name, phone number, hours and website should be the same on your website, Apple Maps, Bing, Facebook and any directory. Old details in one place confuse customers and search engines alike.

## 9. Reviews

Google says the number of reviews and their scores factor into local ranking. Ask every customer the same way after each job, with your review link or a QR card, and reply to every review. Never buy, swap or filter reviews.

## Fixed something?

Changes to a profile can take a while to show on Maps. Check again after a few days, searching the same way as in step 1.
