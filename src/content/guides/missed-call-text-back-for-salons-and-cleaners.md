When you're with a client or on a job, the phone rings out, and the caller may try the next business on the list. Missed-call text-back sends them a short text from your business number, so they know you saw the call and what happens next. This guide covers how it works, what you need, what it costs and how to test it.

## How it works

1. A customer calls your business number and you can't answer.
2. When the call ends unanswered, your phone system sends them a text from the same number, saying it is automatic.
3. The text says what happens next: when you'll call back, or a link to book or ask for a quote.
4. You see the missed call and any reply in one place, and call back.

It only answers someone who just called you. It is not a robocall, not an AI calling people, and not a marketing list.

## Why it suits salons and cleaners

- **Salons and spas:** your hands are busy with a client. A booking link in the text lets the caller book without waiting for you. See [websites for salons and spas](/websites-for-salons-and-spas/).
- **Cleaning companies:** you're on a job, often without a free hand. The text can say when you'll call back and link your price or quote page. See [websites for cleaning companies](/websites-for-cleaning-companies/).

## What you need

- **A business number that can send texts automatically.** Many business phone services offer it. Your provider tells you what set-up or registration the number needs before it can send automatic texts; ask before you choose one.
- **A short, honest message:** who you are, that the text is automatic, and what happens next.
- **One place to see missed calls and replies,** so nobody waits for a call back that never comes.
- **Consent for anything beyond the reply.** Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) treats replies differently from marketing: the CRTC's guidance is that a message sent in response to a request, inquiry or complaint doesn't need consent. Keep the text-back a plain reply to the call. Anything promotional after that, such as an offer, needs the person's consent, and a record of it.

## A sample message

A sample for a fictional salon. Yours uses your name, your hours and your link:

> Hi, this is [your salon]. Sorry we missed your call: this is an automatic text. We're with a client and will call you back today during opening hours. To book now: [your booking link]

Short, plain and true. Don't promise a call-back time you can't keep.

## Test it before it goes live

- Call from a mobile, let it ring out, and check the text arrives from your business number and reads well on a phone.
- Try a phone on another carrier too.
- Call after hours: does the text still make sense then?
- Reply to the text: does the reply reach you?
- Write down how to pause it, for holidays or when you're away.
