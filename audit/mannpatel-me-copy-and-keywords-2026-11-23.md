## 1. What I found

**The site has about 33,000 words of visible copy across 31 pages** (measured from each page's main content, with forms and hidden elements removed). A small service business site needs about 12,000 to 15,000. The length isn't spread evenly:

| Where the words are | Words now | Why it's heavy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six service pages | 10,611 | Each lists what's included twice: a "Clear boundary, written scope" block (290–760 words) and a "What each part includes" block (680–870 words) |
| /pricing/ | 2,625 | Package cards (1,284 words), then a side-by-side table (675 words) repeating the same inclusions |
| Free-check block | 119 × 19 pages ≈ 2,260 | The same four steps, "Not included" line and finder prompt on 19 pages |
| Package detail | 5 to 6 copies each | "Up to five pages, one enquiry path…", "You provide…", "Paid to other providers…", "Timeline…" each repeated on 5 or 6 pages |
| Industry pages | 1,250–1,280 each | 560-word package block copied from the web design page |

**Repeated words and phrases** (counts across the whole site): "written" 121, "short" 101, "scope" 78, "real" 77, "not included" 71, "your name" 65, "most important first" 54, "Toronto and the GTA" 50, "tested" 37, "honest" 20. They're all true, but at this frequency they stop meaning anything. A reader skims past the twentieth "honest".

**What a business owner needs from any page fits in five answers:** what you do, who it's for, what it costs, what they get, and what to do next. Every page should answer those in the first screen and stop once the buyer has enough to act. The detail behind it (exclusions, who pays the domain, timelines) belongs in one place, /pricing/, with a link from everywhere else.

**Recommended target: about 14,500 words, a 56% cut.** Guides and legal pages stay close to their current length because there, length helps. The cuts come from the service, pricing, industry and area pages and the repeated blocks. The per-page plan is in B1-copy-cut-plan.csv.

### Rules for the rewrite (your voice: direct and specific)

1. **One fact, one place.** Inclusions, exclusions, "billed to you" and timelines live on /pricing/. Other pages give 3 to 5 bullets and link there.
2. **Five answers first.** What, for whom, price, what you get, next step, visible on a phone before scrolling.
3. **Cut the trust words, keep the proof.** Delete "honest", "clearly", "plainly", "real" unless they carry a fact. "Every account set up in your name" is proof; "clearly labelled" isn't.
4. **One call to action per section.** Most pages show three CTAs together ("Request a quote / Start with the free check / Get a free website check"). Use one primary CTA (the free check) and one text link.
5. **The free-check block in full only on Home, /contact/ and /free-check/.** Everywhere else, use a one-line banner.
6. **No numbered eyebrows or slogan headings** ("Found, answered, booked.", "Asked before buying."). Use headings that say what's under them; they read better and help search.

### 3.2 Findings

**K1. No keyword stuffing, and none needed.** No page repeats its target phrase unnaturally (the exact phrase appears 0 to 5 times per page). The issue is the opposite: several titles use your words, not the searcher's.

**K2. Searchers say "website designer", "prices" and "cost". Some of your titles don't.** Autocomplete for "web designer scarborough" returns "website designer scarborough", "web design company scarborough" and "web developer scarborough". For Toronto pricing it returns "website design toronto prices" and "how much does website design cost in toronto". Your pricing title is "Web Design & Local SEO Pricing in Toronto". Proposed titles are in B2 (column 7).

**K3. Prices belong in titles.** In the first report, Google AI Mode picked providers whose titles carry a price ("Web Design Scarborough from $300 + $50/mo", "Custom Sites from $890"). Your published prices are an advantage that your titles don't show. Proposed: Scarborough page, Home, pricing, cleaning, salon, Google setup ($650) and Website Care ($149 a month).

**K4. The Home H1 doesn't say Scarborough** ("Websites and Google listings for Toronto small businesses"); only the title does. Proposed H1: "…for Scarborough and Toronto small businesses."

**K5. Google Business Profile setup has clear buyer phrasing and an unanswered question.** Autocomplete: "google business profile setup service / cost / price". People Also Ask: "How much does it cost to set up a Google Business Profile?". Your page answers it ($650) but not under a heading in those words. Add that H2, and put "Google Business Profile Setup" and "$650" in the title.

**K6. Weak or ambiguous demand; don't build more.** "website redesign toronto", "small business website toronto" and "cheap website design toronto" return no autocomplete suggestions. "web designer north york" autocompletes to North Yorkshire. Keep one redesign page, and either rewrite North York with real local facts or noindex it (main report AREA-1). Don't publish the Markham, Pickering, East York or Ajax drafts until you have local work there.

**K7. Overlapping pages compete with each other.** Shared phrasing measured: Scarborough vs North York 59%; web design vs website redesign 49%; the two cost guides 39%; cleaning vs salon 36%. The cut-down copy removes most of it, because the overlap is the repeated package text.

**K8. AI-phrased searches.** Autocomplete shows "how to make chatgpt recommend my business", "how to get chatgpt to recommend my business" and "how to make my business show up on chatgpt". Your guide's title is "Showing Up in ChatGPT & AI Overviews". Proposed: "How to Get ChatGPT to Recommend Your Business".

**K9. A gap worth filling later: contractors and trades.** "website design for contractors" and "construction website design toronto" autocomplete, and AI answers recommend designers for "Scarborough contractor" prompts. You have no trades page. Add a short "Contractors and trades" section to the web design page now, and a dedicated page once you have one trades project to show.

**K10. Case studies can carry "example" searches.** "cleaning company website examples" and "salon website examples" autocomplete. Retitle the case studies, for example "Cleaning Company Website Example: Nest & Rest".

**K11. Where you rank today.** You didn't appear for "website design toronto prices" (creativescope, 2marketing, djcreative, cresoftech and others rank) or "google business profile setup service toronto" (We Are Digital Marketing ranks, among others). Expect title changes to help with AI answers and click-through first. Rankings for these phrases also depend on reviews and links (main report PROOF-1, IDX-1).
